For me, I'm in the camp that I think learning styles have an effect on how we learn, but I don't exactly know how to put that belief into words, much less provide proof to this claim, but I'll try my best. I think I'm probably a visual learner, where I need most activities shown to me in order to be able to replicate them correctly, as my memory isn't the greatest, and just telling me instructions might not always be fully memorized by me. But I definitely think this has affected how I learn, as now I rely heavily on in person discussions, or canvas videos to help me do my tasks. However, I also think it helps me in some way, because visual learning kind of relies on constant, repeated action. So for example, seeing someone do something, then having me repeat that action multiple times is a good way for me to memorize things. Though I guess this method only works for long term assignments or work duties. Other than that, I don't have much to say about learning styles as a concept. I think they're fine, and I don't really think any differently of a person who goes by auditory instruction rather than seeing the task be performed, so that's my statement.
